How to Clean and Care for Outdoor Wicker Furniture

Updated July 2026 · 6-minute read · By the Veranda Wicker Co. product team

Resin wicker is one of the lowest-maintenance outdoor furniture materials there is: no oiling, no sealing, no repainting. What it does need is an occasional wash, a little common sense about chemicals, and five minutes of prep before winter. Here is the complete routine.

Routine Cleaning (Monthly in Season, ~20 Minutes)

  1. Remove the cushions and set them aside. Spot-clean fabric separately with the same soap solution.
  2. Vacuum or dry-brush the weave with a brush attachment to pull dust, pollen and cobwebs out of the gaps before you add water — wet pollen smears.
  3. Wash with mild soapy water. A squirt of dish soap in a bucket of warm water and a soft-bristle brush is all HDPE weave ever needs. Scrub along the direction of the weave.
  4. Rinse with a garden hose on a normal setting. Never use a pressure washer — concentrated jets can cut fibers or force water into the frame.
  5. Towel off pooled water on the frame and let everything air-dry before the cushions go back on.

Removing Mildew, Sunscreen and Stubborn Stains

Mildew doesn't feed on HDPE, but it will grow on the dirt sitting in the weave in damp, shaded spots. For mildew or greenish film, mix 1 part white vinegar to 4 parts water, scrub, and rinse. For sunscreen or food grease, use a drop of degreasing dish soap directly on the spot. For anything that survives both, a magic-eraser-type melamine sponge used gently works on smooth weave — test on a hidden spot first.

Never use on resin wicker: pressure washers, undiluted bleach, ammonia, abrasive powders, steel wool, or solvent cleaners (acetone, mineral spirits). All of them can dull, discolor or weaken the fiber, and solvent damage is not covered by any manufacturer's warranty — including ours.

Cushion Care

Solution-dyed olefin covers unzip and machine-wash cold on gentle; air-dry only — dryer heat shrinks the backing. Between washes, a hose-down and an upright lean against a wall dries them in a couple of hours. Store cushions in a deck box or indoors when you'll be away for weeks: constant cushion-on-furniture contact in wet weather is what breeds mildew, not rain itself.

Winter Care: What Actually Needs to Happen

  • The furniture can stay out. HDPE weave and aluminum frames are unaffected by freeze-thaw cycles. Give pieces a wash before winter so grime doesn't sit all season.
  • Cushions come in. Store them dry, indoors or in a deck box.
  • Covers: optional but useful. A breathable (not plastic-sheet) cover keeps spring cleanup to a five-minute rinse. Vented covers prevent condensation underneath.
  • Glass table tops are the one fragile part — store them flat indoors, or leave the cover loose enough that snow load doesn't press on the glass.
  • Stack and secure light pieces. Stackable chairs and chaises should be stacked low or strapped so winter wind can't send them across the deck.

Fixing Loose or Snapped Weave Strands

A snagged strand is a ten-minute repair, not a scrapped chair. Warm the strand with a hair dryer for 30 seconds so it turns pliable, re-thread it along its original path with needle-nose pliers, and secure the end inside the frame line with a dab of clear outdoor-rated adhesive. Every Veranda collection has matching repair strands available free for the life of the product — snap a photo of the damage and we'll mail the right color.

Natural Rattan Is Different

Everything above applies to synthetic resin wicker. If you own natural rattan or willow wicker, the rules invert: minimal water (wipe, don't soak), keep it out of direct sun and rain entirely, and feed the cane with boiled linseed oil once a year to prevent cracking. If you're not sure which you own, check the fiber ends underneath the seat — natural cane shows wood grain and varies strand to strand; resin fiber is perfectly uniform with a plastic sheen.
