Outdoor Wicker Furniture Buying Guide

Updated July 2026 · 8-minute read · By the Veranda Wicker Co. product team

Outdoor wicker sets that look identical online can differ by a decade in lifespan. The difference is almost never the style — it is the fiber the weave is made from, the metal under it, and the fabric on the cushions. This guide explains the four things to check before you buy, in the order they matter.

In this guide:
  1. Weave material: HDPE resin vs. PVC vs. natural rattan
  2. Frame: aluminum vs. steel
  3. Cushions: olefin vs. acrylic vs. polyester
  4. Sizing your patio, deck or balcony
  5. Reading the warranty
  6. Pre-purchase checklist

1. Weave Material: HDPE Resin vs. PVC vs. Natural Rattan

The single most important spec in outdoor wicker furniture is the weave fiber. Three materials dominate the market:

  • HDPE (high-density polyethylene) resin is the premium synthetic. The fiber is solution-dyed and UV-stabilized during extrusion, so color runs all the way through and it stays flexible in heat and cold. Expect 10–15 years outdoors. Brand names you may see include "all-weather wicker," "PE rattan" and trade names like Viro® or Rehau®.
  • PVC (vinyl) wicker is the budget synthetic. It looks similar new, but the surface coating fades, and the fiber turns brittle and cracks after 2–3 seasons of sun. If a full sectional costs under about $700 new, it is almost certainly PVC.
  • Natural rattan is beautiful and genuinely renewable, but it is an indoor/covered-porch material. Rain swells it, sun dries and splits it, and humidity grows mildew in the weave. Buy it for a sunroom, not a patio.

How to tell HDPE from PVC in a product listing: HDPE sellers say so explicitly ("HDPE," "polyethylene," "PE rattan"). Listings that only say "resin wicker," "all-weather wicker" or "PE-style" without naming the polymer are usually PVC. When in doubt, ask the seller which polymer the fiber is extruded from — a specialist will answer immediately.

2. Frame: Aluminum vs. Steel

The weave hides the frame, and the frame is where cheap sets fail. Powder-coated aluminum is the right answer for outdoor use: it cannot rust, weighs roughly half what steel does (easy to rearrange, easy to move for cleaning), and handles coastal salt air. Steel frames are stronger per dollar but rust from the inside out the moment the coating is scratched — you will see rust bleeding through the weave at the welds in year two or three.

Check the listing for wall thickness if it is published: 1.2 mm aluminum tube is the sturdy standard for seating; 0.8 mm is flimsy. And sit test if you can — a good frame does not creak or flex under an adult.

3. Cushions: Olefin vs. Acrylic vs. Polyester

Cushions fail years before good wicker does, so fabric matters:

  • Solution-dyed acrylic (e.g., Sunbrella®) is the gold standard: 5+ years of fade resistance, easily cleaned, but adds $200–$500 to a set's price.
  • Solution-dyed olefin is the best value and what we use as standard: nearly acrylic-level fade resistance, inherently mildew- and stain-resistant, quick-drying, at roughly half the cost.
  • Printed polyester is what budget sets ship with. The pattern is printed on the surface and fades visibly in one season; foam cores often lack drainage and stay soggy.

Also check the foam: look for quick-dry (open-cell) foam with a drainable mesh bottom panel, and zippered covers so you can wash — and eventually replace — the covers without buying whole cushions.

4. Sizing Your Patio, Deck or Balcony

Measure your space and subtract walkways before falling in love with a set. Rules of thumb we use with customers:

  • Leave 36 inches of walkway around seating areas and 24 inches behind dining chairs when pulled out.
  • A 4-piece conversation set (loveseat, two chairs, coffee table) needs about 10 × 10 ft.
  • A 5-piece L-sectional needs about 10 × 12 ft; add 3 ft on the open side.
  • A 6-seat rectangular dining set needs about 10 × 14 ft including pull-out room.
  • Balconies: measure the short dimension first. If it is under 5 ft, shop balcony-scale sets — full-depth (34″+) seating will block the door.

Tape the footprint on the ground with painter's tape and walk around it for a day. It is the cheapest mistake-prevention tool in furniture buying.

5. Reading the Warranty

Outdoor furniture warranties vary more than the furniture does. Check four clauses:

  1. Duration and what is covered. Quality HDPE sets carry 3–5 years on frame and weave. "1-year limited" on a synthetic wicker set signals the maker expects PVC-grade lifespan.
  2. Outdoor-use exclusions. Some warranties are void unless furniture is covered or stored — which defeats the point of all-weather furniture. Ours covers genuine uncovered outdoor use.
  3. Fade coverage. Solution-dyed weave and fabric should include fade coverage (often 2–3 years). Printed fabrics never include it.
  4. Parts availability. Ask whether replacement cushion covers, glass tops and feet are sold separately. If not, a lost foot glide eventually scraps a $2,000 set.

Pre-Purchase Checklist

Before you buy any outdoor wicker set, confirm:

  • ☐ Weave is explicitly HDPE / polyethylene, not just "resin"
  • ☐ Frame is powder-coated aluminum (or you accept steel's lifespan)
  • ☐ Cushions are solution-dyed olefin or acrylic with zippered covers and quick-dry foam
  • ☐ Footprint fits with 36″ walkways (taped and tested)
  • ☐ Warranty covers 3+ years of uncovered outdoor use, including fade
  • ☐ Replacement parts and cushion covers are available separately
